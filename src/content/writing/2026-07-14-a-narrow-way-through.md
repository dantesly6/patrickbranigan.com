---
title: "A Narrow Way Through"
date: 2026-07-14
excerpt: "The day's work I threw away is the only reason the one prompt worked."
---

In the words of Paul Atreides in Dune: Part Two, "There is a narrow way through."

This has become my mantra — working multiple lines of thought, across multiple bodies of work, all at once. More questions than answers at any given moment. And I'm reframing those questions in ways I wasn't six months ago.

One particular circumstance struck me this week. I was instructed to take on a UX challenge. I spent a day doing so – working out thoughts, with Claude, and exercising my design thinking. I arrived at a set of possible solutions. Then, naturally I went to document them, only to find other solutions had already been considered and specified. The challenge then was I was instructed to execute on the specifications. What had I just done a day's worth of work for then?

It's in these moments where the experienced designer and the inexperienced designer differ. Years ago, I might have battled this. Years ago I might have said, "no, we should do it this way and here's my evidence." I might have said, "here are the many reasons the specified way doesn't work the way it's imagined." But today, I simply said:

"Given the limitations this specified path surfaces, flex the design. Keep the principles that made the prospective flow intuitive, met accessibility, and solved four distinct UX problems — but flex to hit the goal and layer those back in."

Years ago, I'd have expected a hard "no" or a "that's going to be this amount of work" to achieve the prospective solution(s). But today, the power move is, flex the solution so that it meets the constraints, AND THEN layer in the ingredients that make the prospective solution novel, intuitive, and/or magical.

Two perspectives are made happy. One solution serves both. One prompt materialized it.

And the day I thought I'd wasted? That was the whole point. I couldn't have written that prompt without it. The solutions I threw away are the reason I knew which principles to protect. The output was disposable. The thinking that produced it wasn't.

It is in the small moments and narrow paths of design thinking where the interesting work is now happening. Not in the output or the outcome.
