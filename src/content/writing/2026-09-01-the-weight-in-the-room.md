---
title: The weight in the room
date: 2026-09-01
excerpt: AI isn't a tool problem anymore. It's a people problem.
---
Last week I spent a full day in a room with my company's design and engineering teams. We ARE the product team, with the addition of our CEO, at this point. (CEO wasn't there)

We sat around the same table and simply let loose whatever was on our minds. For the first time it became clear to me just how much weight AI is quietly putting on people. Also, shockingly, I spoke less than 10 statements the entire time. I was engulfed with thought while listening to everyone's perspectives.

At one point, one individual let go of something they'd clearly been sitting on for a very long time. It wasn't a complaint exactly, but more like pressure finding an exit. When they finished...they didn't look up. They just stared down at the table. For minutes. Nobody rushed to fill the void. And when they did, this person was still lost in a trance. As if having been to war and back. 

I've read plenty about AI anxiety in the abstract and I've most definitely felt it myself. Job security, relevance, pace, volume, and in this case process. All of it. Watching it explode from someone specific in the same room is a different thing than battling your own head. This is, of course, because we all handle it differently. But I had no idea just how differently, among a small team like ours.

Here's what I believe: AI is dramatically changing our industries, our workflows, our creative processes, and most importantly causing us to battle with our own identities as professionals and craftsman. I believe all this to be true. And I believe I'm pretty well positioned to battle it all in my head. I have been, and successfully so far.

Here's what I don't have: a tidy answer for anyone other than myself at the moment. I don't think this gets solved with a better rollout plan, or a roadmap, or a re-assuring all-hands. The stare wasn't rhetorical. It was someone processing something very heavy in real time.

One thing I've come to pride myself on in my career is helping other designers, engineers and product-minded individuals in times of indecision or despair. I do usually take a minute or two, or an hour or two, just to process before delivering what may or may not end up being helpful. However, I'm always fairly confident in what I deliver.

But this...

This was something different. It was on another personal, emotional level for this individual. For this team. And I'm just not sure, even still, if what I delivered to the folks around me was helpful or sensible.

I believe we'll get there. But I believe it's going to hurt some more still first...
