---
title: Mistaking a prototype for a solution
date: 2026-09-23
excerpt: Sometimes AI enables articulation
---
I recently saw a post on LinkedIn that struck me. Here's an excerpt from it:

--

> *"Designer gets handed a vibe-coded prototype.*
>
> *This is what we want to build! So when can we start?*
>
> *Rookie mistake:*
>
> *Designer takes prototype and starts refining. They identify what doesn't match the design system, and polish the UI until it does. The final output looks flawless. But when launched, the solution barely moves the needle*
>
> *The better approach:*
>
> *The designer ignores the prototype. (If they're sent a prototype link, they don't open it). Designer goes back to first principles and asks: "What's the goal we're trying to achieve?" Based on the answer and evidence presented, designer then decides if the prototype is worth looking at or not."*

--

I respectfully disagree with this for one main reason: they're looking at the vibe-coded prototype as a solution because of its visual fidelity, when they should be looking at it as merely a visualization of someone else's thoughts articulated. 

What I've experienced in this AI world as a designer, with builders all around me, is the ability for thinkers to finally articulate what they once couldn't, in a format that's more universally digestible. 

To not open a prototype merely because of its form factor and fidelity is doing yourself a disservice, in that you're not inviting another lens on the challenge. The best work comes from a myriad of professional thought aligning behind a goal. When the founder, engineer, PM, or other contributor brings to you a high fidelity prototype with AI, they're saying, "This is what I mean!" Your job as a designer is to take that into consideration. It may not be the *right* answer but it may do a world better in distributing to you what the goal is in their mind. The prototype is a piece of the evidence. 

To not look at a non-design and/or engineer prototype would be dismissing potential evidence.
